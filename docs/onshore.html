Constraint

Onshore component is negative. NO-GO when the strongest onshore component anywhere from the surface to 1000 ft reaches the limit.

Sources

    The onshore component is the part of the wind blowing straight in from the ocean, perpendicular to the shoreline at each pad (see Constraint for the shoreline normal). It is negative when onshore. For each model member and hour the most negative value between 10 m and the layer top is kept, using every model level in the layer plus the wind interpolated to the layer top. P(Backup NO GO) uses the backup (model) limit and P(Primary NO GO) the primary (balloon) limit; both are the weighted share of members at or beyond the limit.

    Hi-res models provide 10 m, 80 m and pressure-level winds (1000 to 925 mb); the global ensembles and NBM come through Open-Meteo at 10 to 180 m and pressure levels where available. A model whose highest level is below 80 m is left out. Each source's weight is split across its members, so large ensembles don't outvote the hi-res guidance by count.
